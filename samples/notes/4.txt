On the zettelkasten as a second memory.

Luhmann described his box not as a filing
system but as a conversation partner: a mind
that could surprise him by producing unexpected
connections he had not consciously made.

The surprise was structural. The box held more
than any single reading session could retrieve,
so returning to it meant encountering a past
self whose associations had since diverged.

Niklas Luhmann
"Kommunikation mit Zettelkästen"
1981

Describes the zettelkasten as a cybernetic
system — not a tool for storage but for
generating complexity from simple rules:
one idea per card, every card addressable,
links explicit.

The insight: surprise is a property of
the system, not the user. The box becomes
a second mind precisely because it was
built by a prior self.
